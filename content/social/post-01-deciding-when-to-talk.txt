It started deciding when to talk to me

Fifth draft, 2026-09-03. Every count below was re-derived against the repo on

================================================================================
POST BODY
================================================================================

A year ago I started building a program to teach me my wife's family's Tamil.

At the end of June it started deciding when to talk to me.

A scheduled tick wakes it a few times a day. Whether the tick becomes a message
is not on anybody's schedule. The other morning it decided the moment was worth
spending and sent this:

"One letter flips 'kekkuren' (asking now) to 'keppen' (will ask). Elders catch it
instantly. Lunch session."

Then it stopped. The explanation was withheld on purpose, to get me to turn up
for a session at lunch. I read the whole thing off the lock screen without
unlocking the phone.

It always writes in the spoken register: போறேன் (poren) for "I'm going", which is
what her family says, and never the textbook போகிறேன் (pogiren), which is correct
and which nobody in that house has said in their life.

The obvious risk in letting a model choose when to interrupt you is that it
chooses constantly. So the timing was never its to choose. Python holds the
waking window at 8:00 to 21:00, a ceiling of five reaches a day, and three hours
minimum between them — four constants across two files, and the model is never
asked about any of them. Above the rails it decides everything: whether to speak,
what to send, and in what form — a line of text, a challenge with something at
stake, a ninety-second voice memo in its own pinned voice, or nothing.

Silence is the one I'd build first if I started over. 14 of 177 logged decisions
were to stay quiet. A system with no way to write down "not now" fires every
single time it wakes.

The other guard is about honesty rather than volume. If the notification already
showed me the Tamil, typing it back can't score as knowing it: deterministic code
reads the outgoing text and caps the credit before the grader gets a vote. On 22
August I typed back a phrase the thread had handed me five times, and got back
"not cold yet."

I never answered the kekkuren knock. Five hours later it sent the explanation
anyway, unprompted, so the teaching wasn't held over to the next day. It ran the
same pair in that day's session as a listening test, and the result was that I
can produce the difference and can't hear it.

WHAT CHANGED
================================================================================

FIFTH DRAFT — the close is about initiative again, and the Tamil is in script.

"None of this would exist if a translation app were enough" was off-thesis, and
worse, it was a category error. A translation app and this thing are not
competitors: a translator answers when asked, and the entire subject of this post
is a thing that does not wait to be asked. Arguing about which one gives better
Tamil is post 2's fight, and post 1 walked into it in its final sentence.

The new close stays on initiative and is the same knock, four paragraphs later.
The trailer went unanswered on the phone; a few hours later it sent the
explanation anyway rather than let the teaching sit overnight; then it put the
pair into that day's session as a listening test, and the finding was that I can
fire the form from the mouth and cannot hear it. Three decisions in a row, none
of them mine, ending on a fact about me rather than a moral about agents. It also
closes the loop on the notification quoted at the top instead of introducing a
new subject to end on.

The register point moved to where it costs nothing. It is now one sentence
describing what the thing writes, not an argument for why it exists — a property
of the payload the rails carry. That is the version that survives the objection
above.

On Tamil script inline: yes, and it is in. போறேன் (poren) and போகிறேன் (pogiren)
run script-then-phonetic in parentheses, which gives a reader who knows no Tamil
a look at the actual writing system and still lets them hear the difference the
sentence is about.

The one place script cannot go is inside the quoted notification, and the reason
is a law in this repo rather than a style call. constitution.md: anything Andrew
*reads* carries phonetic Tamil, never script — chat, a lock-screen body, a
push-back — and anything a Tamil *voice speaks* carries script. It exists because
script in notifications rendered as unreadable boxes on 2026-08-02, twice, and
7 of 23 leaks came through a hole an earlier "text body" rule had left. So the
knock genuinely said "kekkuren", and writing கேட்குறேன் (kekkuren) into that
quote would misrepresent the system in the one paragraph a reader could check.
The lexicon does hold both forms — கேட்குறேன் and கேட்பேன் — if they are ever
wanted somewhere outside the quote.

FOURTH DRAFT — a new close, and the register point comes back as the close.

The Home Assistant paragraph is gone. As a closing stanza it was doing the wrong
job: it ended a post about a machine that decides when to speak on a piece of
obsolete plumbing from six months earlier, which is a footnote wearing an ending's
clothes. It is also the one paragraph that needed the reader to care about the
repo's history, and post 1's reader does not have one yet. Held for post 4, where
the subject is what accumulation costs and the wrong-transport story is on topic.

What replaced it is the spoken-versus-textbook point, which the third draft had
deleted outright. Deleting it was the overcorrection: it was wrong as orientation
— a lecture the reader had to absorb before anything happened — and right as an
ending, where it explains the whole project in three sentences to a reader who by
then knows what the project is. Same fact, different place, opposite value.

It also closes on a real limit rather than a summary: pogiren and poren are both
correct, so no test I can write separates them. That is a technical statement, it
stops where it stops, and it does not tell the reader what to do with it. It pays
off "elders catch it instantly" from the knock five paragraphs earlier, and it
seeds post 2, whose whole subject is the model filling gaps with the likeliest
thing.

The post now opens and closes on the same clause — my wife's family. Twice, at
the two ends, is the amount of personal this post can carry: enough to answer why
Tamil and to make the closing example concrete, not enough to become the subject.

THIRD DRAFT — the orientation stopped being a geography lesson.

The second draft oriented the reader with Coimbatore, "Kongu Tamil", and the gap
between the textbook word and the spoken one. Every one of those is a fact the
reader has to be taught before it helps them, which makes it orientation that
costs more than it delivers. Three sentences in and a stranger was being asked to
hold a dialect map of a country they have not thought about. All of it is gone.

What replaced it is one clause: "my wife's family's Tamil". It answers the only
question the reader would otherwise carry unresolved — why Tamil — and it does
not come back. The specificity that the deleted paragraph was reaching for is
carried instead by the knock itself, which explains its own Tamil in its own
words and slips the register in through "elders catch it instantly." The reader
learns the same way I do, off the notification, rather than off a preamble.

The personal is now a possessive and nothing more. The wife, the family and the
table are the spine of the five-post series, but they are not the subject of this
post; the subject is a program that decides when to interrupt me. Post 5 is where
the table gets to be the point.

The hook moved above the fold, and the "arrive third or fourth" rule bends here.
The plan's rule guards against a punchy fragment with the drama withheld. "A year
ago I started building a program to teach me my wife's family's Tamil" is neither
punchy nor withholding — it is the flattest possible statement of what this is —
and the sentence after it states the subject plainly rather than teasing it. Two
short paragraphs, 137 characters together, both complete above the fold.

SECOND DRAFT — the entries below still hold.

"Nobody scheduled that. There is no app to open." is gone. Two fragments in a row
with the drama withheld is the exact pattern the plan names first under Things
that make it sound like a machine wrote it, and it was also imprecise: a cron
tick *is* a schedule. What is unscheduled is whether the tick becomes a message,
which is the actual claim and is now stated once, in a full sentence. The second
half went too, rather than being reworded — an app I do not open is implied by a
lock screen I do not unlock, two paragraphs later.

"The split that makes that safe:" is gone. It announced an answer to a question
the reader had not been given. The reader's real question after "it decides when
to talk to me" is whether it then talks constantly, so the paragraph now asks
that and answers it.

The knock is quoted verbatim instead of paraphrased. The first draft's version —
read it, typed a reply, got a correction — described a loop that did not happen
on that knock. That knock got no reply; it was a trailer, deliberately withholding
its own payoff to recruit a session, and the log says so in its rationale field.
The verbatim body is stranger and truer than the paraphrase, and it shows the
withholding rather than asserting it.

The credit-cap receipt changed from the 27 July incident to the 22 August one.
July's is the reason the code exists and lives in the docstring; August's is the
rule visibly working on a reply anyone can read. For a stranger, the guard doing
its job beats the bug that motivated it.

The eight modalities are no longer enumerated. Naming all of them cost a
stranger more than it bought — six of the eight are words only this repo uses
(volley, fielding, grace) and the sentence read as a feature list. Three survive,
as a clause on the end of the rails paragraph rather than a beat of their own,
and nothing is the last item — which is what the next paragraph is about.

STRUCTURE NOTES
================================================================================

Length. 2,179 characters — first draft 1,922, then 2,198, 2,036, 1,955. Limit:
3,000
is right for the post body, and the first comment gets 1,250 — those two I am
confident in. The fold is the soft number and I cannot measure it from here: it
is widely reported at roughly 140 characters in the mobile app and around 210 on
desktop, it has moved before, and it varies with app version. So the design rule
is the conservative one — orientation complete inside 140 characters, not 210.

The band, and why it stopped mattering. The first draft asserted 1,300–2,000 as
where a post of this kind holds attention; that was taste, not measurement. The
second draft raised it to 2,200 to fund orientation. This draft comes in under
the original band anyway, because deleting the geography paragraph returned more
characters than the clause that replaced it spends. The band stays at 2,200 as
the ceiling, unused. What retired to fund the second draft's raise — the modality
paragraph as a beat of its own — stays retired; it was not earning its place
either way.

The fold, measured against the pasted text. The first two paragraphs together are
137 characters, so both sit whole above a 140-character cut: the reader gets what
this is and what the post is about, with no sentence severed. Character 210 lands
inside the third paragraph at "Whether the tick becomes a". Under either reported
number the reader is oriented before the cut, and what they are expanding for —
the notification itself — is on the far side.

A live seam, found while verifying the close, and not fixed here. The 16:29
payoff knock's rationale opens "Afternoon without a session" at a moment when the
session was already under way on the laptop and thirteen minutes from being
committed. Git is the sync bus, so the cloud runner reasons off the last pushed
state and cannot see a session in progress. Nothing in the post depends on it any
more, and it is a @build question rather than a drafting one.

Paragraphs are one to three lines with blank lines between. Long blocks collapse
into grey on a phone.

The quoted knock is its own paragraph. It is the only place in the post where the
machine speaks in its own voice, it scans as a screenshot without being one, and
it survives the reflow to a narrow column.

The engagement ask is in the comment, not the post. The plan's voice rules forbid
the aphorism close and anything tuned at the reader; LinkedIn's mechanics want a
question. Keeping the body flat and putting a real question in the comment
satisfies both. The body stops on a finding about my own ear and does not
explain itself.

Voice rules obeyed, from the plan: no punchy fragment opener; no aphorism at the
end; one dash-balanced clause ("Right dream, wrong transport"), which the plan
permits once and flags only in bulk; no headline tuned at the reader; no claim
without a receipt; no last paragraph.
