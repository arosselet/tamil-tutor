This morning my phone told me that one letter flips "kekkuren" (I'm asking) to
"keppen" (I'll ask), and that elders catch it instantly. I read it off the lock
screen without unlocking, typed a reply, and got a correction back.

Nobody scheduled that. There is no app to open. A cron tick woke the thing, and
it decided on its own that this was a moment worth spending.

I spent a year building a Tamil tutor so I could answer my wife's family in their
own Kongu Tamil. The part I did not expect to matter most is that it reaches me
first.

The split that makes that safe:

Python owns the rails, and only the rails. WAKING_START_HOUR = 8.
WAKING_END_HOUR = 21. MAX_REACHES_PER_DAY = 5. MIN_GAP_HOURS = 3. Four constants
in one file. The model cannot cross them because it is never asked.

The model owns whether, what and how. Eight forms are available to it: a line of
text, a challenge with something at stake, a sixty-second voice memo in its own
pinned voice, an eavesdrop on two people talking at native speed, and silence.

Silence is the one I would build first if I started over. 14 of 175 logged
decisions were to say nothing. A system with no way to write down "not now"
fires every single time it wakes.

The other guard is about honesty rather than volume. If the notification already
printed the Tamil, typing it back caps at "hinted" — deterministic code reads the
outgoing text, and the grader gets no vote. That rule exists because on 27 July I
replied "Oru nimsham" and was credited for two words that appear nowhere in it.

The ancestor of all this is my favourite thing in the repo. 25 February, 14:52: I
shipped a public RSS feed. 21:35 the same day: I deleted what it replaced, a
contraption where phone-Gemini wrote a JSON blob, I shared it to a webhook, it
parked as a text entity in Home Assistant, and I hoped my laptop would eventually
pick it up. Right dream, wrong transport, seven hours apart.
