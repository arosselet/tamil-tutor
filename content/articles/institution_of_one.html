The class of problem

A goal that nobody is enforcing

Some goals come with an apparatus around them. A degree has a supervisor, a committee, a submission date and an audience that will judge the result in public. Miss six weeks and someone notices.

Other goals have none of that. An adult learning a language they are not immersed in, taking up an instrument at forty, keeping up a rehab protocol after the physiotherapist has discharged them. Four properties travel together:

  • Nothing degrades when you skip. There is no penalty to absorb, and the decision to quit never has to be made out loud. It happens by not happening.
  • The reward is months out and cannot be scheduled. You cannot book the day you first follow the conversation at the dinner table.
  • A single session emits no signal. Nothing you can feel changes in twenty minutes, which reads as nothing happened.
  • Nobody is holding the standards. No teacher to say the register is wrong, no editor to catch the error, no syllabus committee to stop this year's version drifting away from last year's.

That last one is the tell. The absent thing is not motivation specifically — it is an institution: a bundle of separate jobs, held by different people, that a school performs on your behalf so quietly you only notice them once they are gone.

This note is about what software has to become to take that bundle on for one person. Most of it does not port from a demo or a chat wrapper, because most of it is not conversational at all.

It is drawn from one instance: one learner, one language, one repository — the Coimbatore Tamil spoken in my wife's family home, not the written standard that courses teach. Every specific below is real and from that build.

It is worth being plain that none of it arrived in this order, or in this shape. The practice is about a year old. For the first half of that it was not a system at all: a long prompt I pasted into a chat assistant, on my phone or on my laptop, whenever I wanted a lesson. No state, no schedule, no audio, nothing running while I was not looking. The repository is six months old. The part that reaches out on its own is from the end of June. Nothing here has been running unattended for a year — what has been going on for a year is the failing, and that is where all six parts came from.

The order it actually arrived in is close to the reverse of the order this note presents them in. Audio came first, and for a motivational reason before a pedagogical one: the lessons had become a chore and I kept losing the thread, so I turned them into a podcast that could ride along with the washing-up. That was the motivation engine before it had a name or a file. Measurement came last. For months the system knew what I had listened to and nothing about what I could say, and the debriefs I bolted on to close that gap — what did you catch, what did you recognise — never worked, because a person grading their own comprehension is the least reliable instrument in the building. What I can produce cold, with no warm-up and nothing in front of me, only became the primary number recently. It is the number the whole thing is steered by now, and it is much smaller than the one it replaced.

Teaching turned out to be a small fraction of the work.

Where the six came from

Take a school apart and count what falls out

The honest way to arrive at an architecture for this is not to design it. It is to look at the institution you no longer have and ask which of its jobs are now unowned. A language class is a good specimen because its jobs are visibly held by different people, on different clocks, answerable to different things. That separation was there before software was involved.

The teacher's notebook. Who has met what, who is faking it, who needs the thing from three weeks ago again.

The learner model

The timetable, the room, and the tray you put the homework in. Contact at a time you did not choose, and an answer that gets back to the person marking it.

The loop and the rails

The tuition already paid, the grade at the end, the classmate who asks where you were. The reasons you are in the room at all.

The motivation engine

The coursebook and the tape. Material made in advance by people who are not you, aimed at roughly where you are.

The studio

The fact that the teacher actually speaks the language, in the register you need, and can say "nobody says it that way."

The domain pack

The department. The syllabus review, the record of what was tried in 2019 and dropped, the reason next year's course is not a random walk away from this year's.

The governance kit

Six jobs, six owners, and a school keeps them apart for the same reason any system does: they fail differently and are fixed by different people. A registrar cannot fix a bad textbook. A brilliant textbook cannot make anyone show up.

This is a check I ran afterwards, not a history. Four of the six existed as code long before they had names, because they are what you cannot avoid writing to make the thing run at all. Governance and motivation were named last, after the system kept failing in ways the other four could not fix.

The fifth row is the one I could not have written at the start, and how it got its content is the strongest argument in this note for having a named informant. The goal was always to speak my wife's Tamil. I did not know what that sentence committed me to. I did not know that spoken and written Tamil are far enough apart to be separate things to learn, or that the written form is not one thing either. My first dialect instruction said Chennai — not because I wanted Chennai Tamil, but as a bet about what would be best represented in a model's training data, and to be sure I was at least being taught an Indian variety rather than a Sri Lankan one. Coimbatore came later, and it came from a person rather than from a design. When I finally got over myself and showed the thing to my wife, the dialect was the first correction she made: Kongu and Chennai do not sound alike, and the one she would want to hear from me is hers. The target is the real one now. It was arrived at, not specified — and it could not have been specified from the inside, because the gap this part encodes is invisible to anyone who cannot already hear it.

One candidate failed the test and is worth naming. Continuity — remembering what happened last week and what is still open — looked like a seventh part and got a schema of its own: tracked threads, due-ness scores, bookkeeping. It never failed on its own, only ever as a symptom of the learner model. It was cut, and continuity is now a page of prose the coach rewrites at every close. A concern that cannot fail alone is not a part.

The one pair everyone collapses

A motivation engine is not a notification system

Both show up as a message on a phone, so they read as one thing. They are separated by the clearest evidence in the build: they fail in opposite directions, and are debugged with different instruments.

  • Perfect rails, no motivation engine. Doses arrive on time, on the right channel, correctly rate-limited, scored properly whenever they are answered. Nobody answers them. Every log is green. This is the state most personal-agent projects reach and mistake for a delivery bug.
  • Perfect motivation engine, no rails. Genuinely compelling reasons to practise that never reach anyone at 7am — and when a person does reply, the answer lands nowhere and moves nothing.

Rails questions are answered in an afternoon with timestamps: did the tick fire, did the gate pass, did the reply get matched to its prompt. Motivation questions are only answerable over weeks: what happened after the four-day lapse, which format is quietly never answered, whether the person came back on their own.

The six

What each one is, and the failure that paid for it

Each entry states what the part needs and why, then one thing that actually went wrong. Every mechanism named here replaced something that broke in a way I can point at.

Learner model

Stands in for: the teacher's notebook

What it believes about the person

One store, written by one path, read by everything else.

  • Two grades, never one score. Recognising something and producing it cold are different states that decay at different rates. Collapse them and the system reports 92 words solid while the person can fire 41. That gap is the entire problem in adult language learning, and it has an analogue anywhere: what you can follow, versus what you can do unaided.
  • One ordering function, not a convention. What comes next is computed in one place and read by every channel. It is shared code because it began as hand-copied sort keys in three callers, and the copies drifted.
  • An error ledger that retires rather than deletes. Record the pattern being repeated, not each wrong answer, and reopen the original entry intact when it recurs. A mistake you believed was fixed coming back is the most informative event the ledger can hold.
What went wrong — silence is not one thing

An access token expired and the inbound reply path died. Every reply the learner typed was rejected upstream, and nothing appeared in the repository at all — no failed run to find, because no run was ever created. Outgoing messages kept arriving normally, which is why it took three days to see.

The outage was not the expensive part. Each unanswered prompt was recorded as reply: null — byte-identical to a prompt the learner had read and ignored. Three downstream signals consume that field, so for three days the system quietly wrote a portrait of a non-responsive learner and began steering selection by it. A broken channel and a person who declined must never serialise the same way.

Not the transcript. Conversation history is what was said; this is what is believed, and only a scored answer may change it.

Deeper: You Knew This on Tuesday

Loop and rails

Stands in for: the timetable, the room, the homework tray

How a dose reaches them, and how the answer gets home

The largest surface, and the part where an agent most visibly behaves like one.

  • Policy in the model, rails in code. The model decides whether to reach out, how, and when. Code holds the waking hours, the daily cap and the minimum gap, and nothing else. Split it the other way, with rules judging relevance and prompts holding limits, and you get a system that is both rigid and unreliable.
  • Silence is a first-class output. "Not now" has to be a decision the system can make and record. If it cannot, it will always fire.
  • Credit is capped in code, not by the judge. If the outgoing message showed the answer, the best verdict available is "hinted", enforced deterministically. Outreach must never be able to fake evidence about the person.
What went wrong — two questions, one answer

Two scheduled messages came due in the same drain tick and both fired. The reply judge resolved an incoming answer by taking the most recently sent message as the question — a shortcut that had been safe when only one message could ever be outstanding. So a correct answer to the first was graded against the second, and marked wrong. The learner model moved backwards on a rep the person had genuinely earned.

Neither half of the fix was a prompt. The queue now sends at most one unforced message per tick, and every outgoing message carries an id that the reply is required to come back with, so an answer finds its own question instead of the newest one.

Not the motivation engine. This part is transport and accounting: did it arrive, did the answer get home, did it score honestly. It is indifferent to whether anyone wants it.

Deeper: It Texts You First

Motivation engine

Stands in for: the tuition, the grade, the cohort

Where the pull comes from

Devices that make a payoff months out feel present-tense.

  • No manufactured consequence. Streaks and guilt invent a cost for missing, which makes missing expensive, which makes quitting cheaper than returning. The design goal is the opposite: make returning cheap. No makeup work, no deficit recited back, a partial session counts.
  • Assignments that leave the software. One line to deploy in real life tonight, unprompted, collected at the next contact. A line that survives live use is the strongest evidence of real capability available, and it makes a distant goal briefly concrete.
  • An honest boundary. Software substitutes for deciding to start, choosing what to work on, remembering where you were, and making progress visible. It does not substitute for consequence or desire. A system that pretends otherwise becomes a nag and gets muted.
What went wrong — the assignment that got refused

I sent him out with a line to deploy at dinner: a remark about handling chilli well. He refused it, on two grounds, both correct. No audience — it is a line for a table of relatives meeting you, and I had aimed it at a wife who has watched him eat for ten years and would only find it odd. No teach — the phrase had never been shown to him, so a mission was smuggling in a cold quiz on unseen material.

Two rules now hold: a line must be taught before it can be assigned, and its audience has to be someone who would plausibly hear it. The replacement rode something guaranteed to happen instead — say paravaayilla, bare, the next time she apologises for anything at all. It fired that evening, one day after the word was first taught. The device was never the problem; the aiming was.

Not notifications, gamification, or persona. It is not a tone of voice. It is a set of constraints every other part obeys, which is why it appears as a frame in the diagram and not a box.

Deeper: Nothing Gets Worse If You Skip Today

Studio

Stands in for: the coursebook and the tape

How the material gets made

Generating content that ships without a human reading it first.

  • Narrow passes, not one prompt. Plan, then script, then dialect and integrity. A single generation cannot hold all the constraints and stay accurate. Collapsing the passes here corrupted the output twice, in two different ways, and both versions read fluently.
  • The writer gets no filesystem. Generation runs sandboxed and print-only: no file writes, no version control, no publishing. A deterministic layer receives the text, saves it, checks it, and decides whether it ships.
  • Variety is a gate, not taste. Register, form and dramatic ingredient are chosen deterministically before generation begins and forbidden from repeating a recent value. A generator asked repeatedly for something fresh converges, and taste is the thing that converged.
What went wrong — the episode that never said it

Each episode is commissioned against an explicit payload: the specific phrases this week's errors require rehearsed. One came back beautifully made — right register, right scene, on topic, and it discussed the situation the target phrase belongs to without ever once saying the phrase. Twelve minutes of audio built to teach something it never uttered.

The learner cannot catch that. Noticing an absent phrase in a language you do not yet have is precisely the capability the episode exists to build. So the check is mechanical and sits before publication: every commissioned phrase must appear verbatim in the script or the run stops. That is the pattern for all of them — each check in the pipeline exists because something specific got through once, and none of them is a sentence added to a prompt.

Not the domain pack. This part governs how material is made and what stops it shipping. It has no opinion about what is correct — it asks the pack.

Deeper: The Writer Gets No Filesystem

Domain pack

Stands in for: a teacher who actually speaks it

What the model does not know about your domain

A vetted delta between what a model produces natively and what the domain actually sounds like.

  • It is curated content with an owner, not a prompt instruction. Versioned like code, reviewed by someone who knows, and applied where it is guaranteed rather than requested — the pronunciation fixes live at the audio renderer, past the point a model could override them.
  • The informant policy is part of the pack. Who the native speaker is, how they are consulted, and what they must never be turned into. Here that is sixty-second checks on specific phrasings, never grammar lessons, because turning a family member into an examiner costs more than it buys.
  • This is the one part you rebuild. Point the architecture at another language, or at another subject entirely, and five of the six come with you as they are — the store, the loop, the motivation constraints, the generation pipeline, the governance kit. The pack does not. It is written again from scratch for the new domain, by somebody who knows that domain, and there is no generating your way around it.
What went wrong — three ways a translator is confidently useless

Morphology. Ask any translator for "I'm going" and you get போகிறேன், pogiren. It is correct, and nobody in Coimbatore says it. The spoken form is போறேன், poren. This is not slang shaved off a proper word — it is the entire register, and a learner drilled on the first one sounds like a newsreader at a kitchen table.

Vocabulary that is right and wrong at once. "We went to the market" comes back with சந்தை for market. Impeccable, and it marks you instantly as someone who learned from a book. In a real sentence the English word stays and the Tamil grammar wraps it: market-ku ponom. English carries the logistics, Tamil carries the meaning-bearing verb. No translator will do that to itself, because to a translator it looks like a defect.

Pragmatics, which is not language at all. Ask for "hello" and you get வணக்கம், vanakkam. The right word and the wrong move: nobody greets family with it. You walk into the house and the greeting is saaptingala? — did you eat. The food question is the hello. A learner armed with the dictionary answer is fluent and instantly foreign.

All three are invisible to every check in the system, because each one is correct. They are only visible to a person who speaks it, which is why the informant is not a nice-to-have and why this part cannot be automated.

Not prompt engineering. A prompt is a request the model may or may not honour. A pack is content with a version and an owner, applied at a point the model does not control.

Deeper: Nobody Actually Talks Like That

Governance kit

Stands in for: the department and the syllabus review

How the system is allowed to change

What keeps a weekly-changing system compounding instead of churning.

  • A decision log that records refusals. What was decided against, and why. A rejected approach leaves no trace in the code, and a fresh model context cannot tell "we tried this and it failed" from "nobody has tried this". It will re-propose the idea, persuasively. So will you, six months on.
  • Budgets with teeth. Prose, code and static-analysis findings all carry ceilings checked in CI. A raise has to ride in the same commit as the growth and name what it retired. A file that keeps hitting its ceiling is doing two jobs.
  • Deliberate latency between an idea and a change. When implementation is nearly free, the scarce resource is evidence. Ideas wait until a pattern reproduces; one-offs never become mechanisms.
What went wrong — a mechanism proposed before anyone read the file

One message format took over the outreach channel: four in four consecutive days, all the same kind, all the same shape. My first proposal was a quota — cap that format at N per week — and I offered it before opening a single file.

Reading the plumbing found two causes, neither of them volume. One line in the prompt expressed a preference that funnelled every message of that type into the same narrow vein. And the outreach memory rewarded the format for converting, which fed it straight back to itself. The better half of the real fix was a deletion. The quota would have capped the symptom, left both causes running, and looked like it worked.

Not tests or code review. Those check whether a change works. This checks whether it should exist, and whether the system is still the same system after six months of them.

Deeper: You Already Decided This

The proof they are separate

Change the goal, and watch which parts collapse

If motivation were really a flavour of the outreach loop, you could not take one of them to nearly zero and leave the other elaborate. You can. Point the same architecture at a research assistant for a doctoral student and the motivation part almost vanishes — not because the work is easier, but because the apparatus is still there: an advisor, a funding clock, a public judgement on a date. Every one of the other five still has real work to do.

A correction worth making in public

For a while I described the motivation work as an accommodation for my own attention, and that framing was wrong in a way that mattered: it made the most portable part of the build look like a personal quirk. The table says it better. The amount of motivation engineering a system needs is set by the structure of the goal, not the wiring of the person. A goal with a date and an audience needs almost none — for anyone. The doctoral student is not more disciplined than the language learner; they are more surrounded.

Software cannot supply consequence or desire. It can supply everything an institution was doing around them — and that turns out to be most of what an institution was doing.

In practice

Which part is broken

The reason to hold the six apart is not tidiness. It is that when something feels wrong, the symptom tells you which one to open — and the wrong instinct, every time, is to reach for the persona.

  • "It asked me something I nailed on Tuesday." Learner model A write that did not land, or two grades collapsed into one. Not forgetfulness.
  • "Nothing came all day, then two things at once." Loop and rails The gate, the cap or the tick. Answerable from timestamps in an afternoon.
  • "It all arrives fine. I just stopped opening it." Motivation engine The only symptom no log will show. Look at weeks, and at what happened after the last lapse.
  • "Great episode. It never actually said the phrase it promised." Studio A missing check, not a weak prompt. Every corruption becomes a gate that stops the run.
  • "It is all correct, and nobody speaks like that." Domain pack The model's native register leaking through. Needs the informant, not more instruction.
  • "We keep re-deciding the same thing." Governance kit The refusal was never recorded, so the idea comes back persuasive and unlabelled.

The order

If you are building one

The parts are separable, but not equally urgent, and some are far more expensive to retrofit than others.

  1. Write down what the system will believe about the person, before any prompt. Two axes at minimum. This is the schema everything else reads, and the expensive thing to change later.
  2. Make the write path deterministic. State changes go through code, never through a model editing a file. The model chooses; code records.
  3. Build one channel end to end, including the return path. A dose that goes out and an answer that comes back scored is the whole loop in miniature. Adding channels afterwards is cheap; retrofitting a return path is not.
  4. Put governance in before there is anything to govern. The decision log and the budgets cost nothing on day one and are nearly impossible to backfill.
  5. Decide how much motivation engineering the goal actually requires. Ask what would happen if the person stopped for a month, and who would notice. If the answer is nobody, this decides whether the system is still running in six months.
  6. Generate content last, and get a real informant before you do. It is the most impressive part and the least load-bearing. It needs the model to aim it, the checks to make it trustworthy, and the pack to make it correct.

One rule sits under all six: nothing goes in that is not earned by a specific outcome or a specific piece of evidence. The pieces that felt clever and had nothing behind them are the ones that got cut.
