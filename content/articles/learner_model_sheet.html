Two stores, two owners · the split everything else depends on

■Counterslexicon.json 336 records, one per word/chunk/frame. Only a scored answer may move a counter. Deterministic, machine-owned, no prose.
□Notebooklearner.json · profile.md What the coach has concluded: how this person wants to be taught, what they called too much, which assignment they called contrived. Prose. Nothing in it could have been produced by a grade.
■One writersync_state.py Every other module reads. Ten scripts import the IO layer; exactly one mutates state. Hand-editing the JSON is banned because the canonicaliser lives in the writer.
The law: a preference is not a result. A system with only counters will keep being correct at someone in a way they do not come back to. Build both stores on day one — the notebook is not a nice-to-have, it is the half that keeps the person.

The record · the whole schema — copy this and you have the pillar's spine

■«key»தமிழ் script Canonical form is the key. A phonetic-only token can never mint a record — one regex decides. Fork to another language, replace the regex.
■phonetic[str] Every spelling the learner might type. Feeds a reverse index so vandhutten resolves to the script key. Never make a learner fight a keyboard.
■recognitionsolid | comfortable | struggled Can they follow it when it arrives.
■productioncold | hinted | none Can they produce it unaided. cold = instant, no prompt. hinted = needed a nudge. Separate axis, separate decay.
■directionfire | catch Whether this item is ever to be forced to production. catch items are ear-only — the win is recognition and demanding output is a bug. 12 of 83 here.
■typechunk | pattern A fixed phrase deployed whole, or a slot template that generates. Patterns are graded on a novel fill, never a memorised line.
■last_surfaceddate Drives every staleness calculation. null sorts as infinitely overdue, so a never-touched item outranks a merely old one.
■seen_in[episode-id] Where it has been heard. Distinguishes exposed from tested — the gap that makes teach-first enforceable.
■repsint Times deliberately worked. Feeds the stuck-flag, not a score.
□pairs_with«key» His line and her answering line, drilled as a unit — recognition alone isn't the win for a call-and-response.
Cheap and load-bearing: direction and type are one word each and they change what the whole system is allowed to demand. Most tutors have neither, then force output on things that were only ever meant to be understood.

Collapse them and you get a lie. One score would report ~92 words solid while the person can actually fire 41. They decay at different rates and they are different skills — what you can follow versus what you can do unaided. The gap is not an error bar; it is the entire work.

What may move a counter · and how far

■a scored answerThe only input. Not a transcript, not attendance, not a tap.
■demotion = one step solid → comfortable → struggled, and struggled is the floor. Never straight to the bottom on one miss — a bad day is not amnesia.
■cold is one-wayProduction going cold is graduation: the item leaves the drill rotation for good and is simply used.
□scaffolded ≠ fired If the prompt contained the answer, or named the move, it logs as seen. The grader has to be honest against its own prompt — this is the rule most often broken, and it is broken by the coach, not the learner.
Build note: route every learner-facing write through the same resolver. We have one flag that does a plain dict lookup instead, so the same word is accepted by one command and rejected by the next. It costs a retry every time and it has never been worth the saved line.

The ordering function · one place, read by every channel — chat, audio, notifications

■RECOGNIZED{comfortable, solid} Only already-recognised items are production targets. You cannot force output on something never understood — that is the teach path, a different door.
■PROD_ORDERhinted 0 < none 1 Ripest first: hinted is one hint away from cold.
■RECOG_ORDERsolid 0 < comfortable 1 Among equals, the better-recognised word is the riper target.
■FOCUS_SIZE12 Dense rotation size. The rest ride a slow background — stored membership, not an emergent sort, so it is immune to counting bugs.
■STUCK_REPS10 Past this, flag the approach, never evict the word. Measured: median 2 reps to cold, p90 5. At 10 it is the method failing, not the learner. A silently parked word is the starvation you are trying to fix.
■RETEST_DAYS14 Hinted items silent this long resurface for a cold retest. Without it hinted is a no-way door — cold graduates, none gets drilled, and the middle state quietly rots.
■INTERVAL_DAYScold 21 · hinted 10 · none 5 Resurfacing clock, keyed on the production axis — the weaker the production, the tighter the loop.
The law: one function, one file, every channel. The moment two surfaces each decide what is due, they disagree, and the disagreement shows up as the system contradicting itself in front of the person.

The error ledger · retires, never deletes

■row tag · said · want · note · date · lane · dose_channel — record the pattern, not the wrong answer. The tag is the unit; the same tag reused is the signal.
■PATTERN_COUNT = 2One is noise, two is signal. Same bar the system uses for its own bugs.
■RETIRE_DAYS = 21Deliberately equal to the cold resurfacing interval, so a pattern and a word age on one clock rather than two constants drifting apart.
■closed by firing A correction never closes a slip. It closes only when the right form fires unaided, later, in a situation that did not hand it over. Retire ≠ delete: recurrence reopens the original entry intact.
■escalate flagMaterial was built for this and they missed it again → change the format, never loop harder.
■commission gate A live pattern with no material ever built for it refuses the session close (hard exit). Override is allowed but must state a reason, on the record.
Why this is the highest-value store: a mistake you believed was fixed coming back is the most informative event the ledger holds. Delete on close and you destroy exactly that signal.

Scar: the whitelist that ate the closes · delete-on-omit

The notebook is rewritten from a fixed key list on every update. slip_closes was not on the list — so recording a close wrote it, and the very same command's rewrite deleted it. No slip had actually closed for a week, and nothing surfaced the loss, because a wiped close is byte-identical to never having tested.
◧the fixA test asserts the round-trip: write the value, run the update that follows it, assert it survived. Not the parse — the round-trip is the assertion that earns its keep.
□the ruleIf you rebuild a record from a whitelist, every new field must be added to it. Prefer a merge that preserves unknown keys — the whitelist bought us nothing that a schema check wouldn't.

Scar: silence is not one thing · the three-day false portrait

An expired token killed the inbound reply path while outgoing messages kept arriving. Every unanswered prompt serialised as reply: null — byte-identical to a prompt the person read and ignored. Silence is not neutral downstream: it feeds decay, demotion and selection. So the system spent three days building, and acting on, a portrait of a non-responsive learner who had in fact replied.
□the ruleA broken channel and a person who declined must never serialise the same way. Give the model a distinct "no inbound event of any kind" state, and a liveness check that can tell them apart.

Not this · boundaries that stop the pillar swallowing its neighbours

□Not the transcript≠ history History is what was said; this is what is believed. Neither store is a record of the conversation, and the conversation is not a substitute for either.
□Not the motivation engine≠ pull This pillar holds no streak, no debt, no makeup work. It reports honestly and says nothing about whether the person should feel bad — that constraint belongs to a different frame.
□Not the curriculum≠ what to teach It orders what is ready. What is worth learning at all is a domain question and lives with the domain pack.

If you are building one · in this order — each step is usable before the next exists

  1. One record, two axes. Key, aliases, recognition, production. Resist a single score; you cannot recover the two later from data collected as one.
  2. A resolver. Alias → canonical, in one function, used by every write. Do this before you have many records and it stays honest.
  3. Staleness. last_surfaced plus an interval per production level. This alone gives you a working "what's due".
  4. One ordering function. Before a second channel exists. Adding it after two channels already guess is a migration, not a feature.
  5. The error ledger. Tags, not wrong answers; a close that only an unaided success can write. Cheap now, impossible to backfill.
  6. The notebook. A prose store the coach writes and the counters cannot. Last to build, first to matter when the person decides whether to come back.
Sequencing note: steps 1–2 are irreversible-if-skipped; 3–5 are additive; 6 can start as a single text field. Nothing here needs a database.
