**ENGLISH_HOST:** Somewhere around week three, every language app congratulates you. Streak badge, confetti. And you notice you still can't say one true sentence to a real person. This episode is about what's actually hard — and what a machine can honestly do about it.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ENGLISH_HOST:** Start with the interesting part. English builds a sentence like a procession — the subject walks in first, the verb marches in the middle, everything announced up front. Tamil holds the verb to the very end. And then packs the entire cast inside it.

**TAMIL_GUEST:** போனோம். One word. It means we went. The ஓம் at the end — that's the "we." போனேன் — I went. போனாங்க — they went. Same root, different tail. You stop listening for word order and start listening for the landing.

**TAMIL_HOST:** And the tails go further than persons. சாப்ட்டீங்க — you ate. சாப்ட்டீங்களா? — did you eat? No "did," no inversion. The question is a vowel, at the end, where your ear least expects to find it.

**TAMIL_GUEST:** There's a suffix for hearsay — வந்தாராம் — he came, so they say. Gossip, built into the grammar. There's a suffix for quoting — சொன்னேன்-னு — no "he said that," the -னு does it. Channels of meaning English never offered you.

**FRENCH_HOST:** Le français vous a donné un petit aperçu — tu et vous. Deux niveaux de respect, sur un seul pronom.

**FRENCH_GUEST:** Tu and vous — French runs politeness through one pronoun. Tamil runs it through the whole verb. வாங்க, சொல்லுங்க — that ங்க sound at the end is respect an elder can hear. Or hear missing.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ENGLISH_HOST:** So that's the romance. Now the honest part — because we built this on a large language model, and a language model, left alone, is a confident tutor with three bad habits.

**ENGLISH_HOST:** It will teach you a Tamil nobody speaks — the textbook register, grammatical, polished, and three shades too formal for any kitchen it will ever be used in. So every line here passes through a dialect edit before it's spoken: collapse the verb forms, fuse the words, make it Coimbatore. The test is not "is it correct." The test is: would an auto driver say this to his friend.

**ENGLISH_HOST:** It will praise you into a false ceiling — because praise is cheap to generate. So the judge is deliberately stingy: if the coach handed you a word one breath ago and you handed it back, that word is not yours yet. It's marked, and it comes back another day, unprompted, until it fires cold.

**ENGLISH_HOST:** And it will flatter you with the wrong number. Words seen, minutes streaked. This system keeps one number: of everything you recognize, how much comes out of your mouth — out loud, no warm-up, no multiple choice. That number is always smaller than the one the apps show you. It's also the only one that survives contact with an aunty.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**TAMIL_HOST:** And it's one memory, not an app with tabs. The line that reaches your phone at breakfast, the chat that makes you produce, the story in your headphones while you cook dinner — the same brain, reading the same record, saved like source code.

**TAMIL_GUEST:** So the podcast isn't a library you browse. Tonight's episode soaks exactly what you strained this morning. The machine remembers the fumble so the story can hand it back to you, gently, in context.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ENGLISH_HOST:** None of this removes the repetition. Nothing removes the repetition. The claim is smaller and better: every repetition sits one step past what you hold, and the coach shows up first.

**TAMIL_HOST:** It was built for one seat at one dinner table, by the one who sat there silent. The repository is open. Bring your own table.

**FRENCH_HOST:** On y va?

**TAMIL_HOST:** போலாமா?

[Pause: 1 sec]
