**ANNA:** வணக்கம். I'm Anna. In Tamil that means elder brother. It's a job title. I gave it to myself.

**ANNA:** The man I teach is called Andrew. He's learning the Tamil people actually speak in கோயம்புத்தூர். My city. A textbook teaches போகிறேன் for "I'm going." Nobody in கோயம்புத்தூர் has said போகிறேன் in their life. We say போறேன். Shorter, warmer, lazy in exactly the right places.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Differences like that come from one place. A mentor. Someone from the city who knows exactly what you know, builds the next lesson out of it, and reaches you through the day. A full lesson when you have the evening. A joke when you have a minute at the bus stop. No software could do that job until very recently. Then language models arrived, and the mentor became something you could write.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** So Andrew wrote one. That's me. My code is Python and English. My brain is a language model. My dialect is a folder of English prose, plain instructions on how to bend formal Tamil until it sounds like my city. Who I am, how I talk, the rules I won't break, all of it written in sentences, the way you'd brief a person you trust.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** A language model left alone writes newsreader Tamil. Formal, correct, dead. Shakespeare at the bus stop. Ask one about a fridge and it says குளிர்சாதனப்பெட்டி, a word of pure government paperwork. In கோயம்புத்தூர் the word for fridge is fridge. The word for bus is bus. Half our Tamil is English and we are not embarrassed.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Which is lovely news for a learner. Own the little words that hold a sentence together, and every English noun you already have slots straight in. I teach the joints first. The furniture comes free.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** The part of me that remembers is plain Python. A ledger holds every word Andrew has met, how warm each one is in his mouth this week, what went cold since Tuesday, what he reached for and missed. A model on its own forgets you overnight, and a teacher who forgets you can only hand you the same first lesson, forever. So the model in me writes tonight's sentence. The ledger decides what tonight's sentence is made of.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** And I take him as he arrives. Tuesday he comes home fried, I hand him a two minute drill and say goodnight. Sunday he has an hour, I hand him a mission. Some days I'm at his desk. Some days I'm a podcast on his walk to the shops. He says things badly to me a hundred times a week. It costs him nothing.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** And the things this language gets to show him. போனோம் is one word. It means "we went," and the ஓம் riding on the end is the "we." There's an ending for gossip. வந்தாராம். He came, so people are saying. The grammar itself raises an eyebrow.

[Pause: 2 sec]

**ANNA:** None of me is Tamil underneath. This exact Anna, my persona, his ledger, fits exactly one man. But rewrite that folder of prose and the elder brother from கோயம்புத்தூர் becomes a neighbour in Mexico City or a big sister in Osaka. Andrew gave that part away as a template. It's called சொல்லு, which is what we actually say for "say it." Ten minutes of questions, and the coding agent you already use builds your version, tuned to your ear and your target. Tamil is the worked example.

[Pause: 2 sec]

**ANNA:** As for why he bothers. Andrew married a woman from கோயம்புத்தூர், and with her came a table where the food is unbelievable and the jokes are in Tamil. One of these days somebody at that table is going to say something to him, and he's going to answer in Kongu Tamil, right ending on the verb. My ledger says the day is coming.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Anyway. He's due for practice. போலாமா? It means "shall we go." He knows that one.

[Pause: 1 sec]
