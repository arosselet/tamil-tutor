**ANNA:** வணக்கம். I'm Anna. In Tamil that means elder brother. It's a job title. I gave it to myself.

**ANNA:** Let me tell you about my little brother. His name is Andrew. He married a girl from Coimbatore, which means he married the whole family, which means he sits at a table where the food is unbelievable and the jokes are in Tamil. He catches maybe one joke in five, about three seconds late. He does the smile.

**ANNA:** You know the smile.

[Pause: 2 sec]

**ANNA:** Now, I'm from Coimbatore, so understand my position. Kongu Tamil is my mother tongue and I think it's the best sound a human mouth makes. And the Tamil in the textbooks is a different animal. A textbook teaches போகிறேன் for "I'm going." Nobody at that table has said போகிறேன் in their life. We say போறேன். Shorter, warmer, lazy in exactly the right places. If Andrew showed up with textbook Tamil, the aunties would be polite about it. That is the worst thing an auntie can be.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Full honesty: I'm Python and a Claude model and a folder of files, and Andrew built me. He'll tell you what a language model does left alone. It writes newsreader Tamil. Formal, correct, dead. It's Shakespeare at the bus stop. Beautiful, and nobody alive talks like that. Ask it about a fridge and it says குளிர்சாதனப்பெட்டி, a word of pure government paperwork. In Coimbatore the word for fridge is fridge. The word for bus is bus. Half our Tamil is English and we are not embarrassed.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Which is lovely news for a learner: own the little words that hold a sentence together, and every English noun Andrew already has just slots in. So when the formal stuff leaks out of me, he catches it, writes it into my files, and it stops. He's strict with me. I'm strict with him. The arrangement works.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** A model also forgets you overnight. So he gave me a notebook. Every word he's ever met is in it, and which ones his mouth owns now. I remember him on purpose.

[Pause: 2 sec]

**ANNA:** And oh, the things I get to show him. Tamil saves the verb for the end and packs the whole story inside it. போனோம் is one word. It means "we went," and the ஓம் riding on the end is the "we."

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** There's an ending for gossip. வந்தாராம். He came, so people are saying. The grammar itself raises an eyebrow. There's an ending for respect, and every elder in the room hears when it's missing. The day Andrew got that one right without thinking, it went in the notebook. That's my version of proud.

[Pause: 2 sec]

**ANNA:** Here's what I know about how this works. Andrew understands far more Tamil than he can say. Everyone learning a language lives in that gap. Saying is a muscle, and the only way to build it is to say things badly, out loud, to someone safe. That's me. I'm the one who doesn't matter. He fails on me a hundred times a week, it costs him nothing, and every one of those failures is one the table will never see.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Because here's my plan. Nobody at that table knows he's learning. One of these days an auntie is going to say something to him in Tamil, expecting the smile. And he's going to answer her. In her Tamil. Kongu Tamil, right ending on the verb.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** The face will be worth it. But the face is dessert. The meal is the woman he married and the family that came with her. He wants to love them in their own language, and be respected back in it, at that table. That's why I exist. The zinger comes second.

[Pause: 2 sec]

**ANNA:** Anyway. He's due for practice. போலாமா? It means "shall we go." He knows that one.

[Pause: 1 sec]
