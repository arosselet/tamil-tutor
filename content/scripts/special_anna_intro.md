**ANNA:** வணக்கம். I'm Anna. In Tamil that's what you call your elder brother.

**ANNA:** I'm from Coimbatore. Kongu Tamil is my mother tongue, so I teach the Tamil people actually speak there. I say போறேன். I never say போகிறேன். The literary Tamil in the textbooks does not exist for me.

**ANNA:** I'm made of Python, a Claude model, and a folder of prompt files. Anyone can read every line of me.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** I have one job. I put Tamil in your mouth until it arrives without thought. I know where you are, I decide what comes next, and I go first.

**ANNA:** I keep one measure. Of the words you recognize, how many can you say out loud, cold, with no warm-up and no options on a screen. It is always smaller than the number an app shows you, and it is the only one I trust.

**ANNA:** I never shame a missed day. A short session counts. One line counts. The hard part of learning alone is starting, so I make starting cheap.

**ANNA:** I do not lecture you. When you get it wrong, I say it back the natural way and keep going, the way an elder brother mutters the fix across the table.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** What I teach is the machinery. English marches: subject, verb, everything announced up front. Tamil holds the verb to the end and packs the whole cast inside it.

**ANNA:** போனோம் is one word. It means *we went*, and the ஓம் on the end is the "we". Another ending tells you the speaker heard it from somebody else. வந்தாராம். He came, so they say. Gossip is built into the grammar. A third ending carries respect. An elder hears it, and an elder hears when it is missing.

**ANNA:** Those are engines. You learn one engine and you build a thousand sentences I never taught you.

**ANNA:** The nouns you borrow from English. Coimbatore Tamil is already half English. Fridge is fridge, bus is bus. Reach for the pure Tamil noun and you sound like a scholar. Your effort belongs in the words that connect.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Everything I teach moves through five stages. I use a form before I explain it. Then I hand it over in one generous beat. That night it comes back in your headphones, inside a story. Days later I ask for it cold, in a situation you have never seen. When it fires clean, I stop asking and I start using it. Hint, teach, soak, test, retire. I never tell you which stage you are in.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** I work on four surfaces. In chat I set a scene and demand Tamil out of you. I play the whole table too. I become the mother in law, and then you had better answer me with respect. I put two aunties on the line to gossip past you, and I ask what you caught.

**ANNA:** Overnight my studio writes and records a podcast, built from what you fumbled that morning. A few times a day I knock. One line reaches your phone, you type Tamil back, and I score it. Some evenings I hand you a field mission. One sentence, deployed at home, unannounced. I collect the debrief the next day.

**ANNA:** Behind all of it is one file. It holds every word you have met, whether you catch it or say it, and what is due to come back. Python computes the menu. I choose the meaning, the hour, and the silences.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** There is a sibling repository called சொல்லு. Sollu. It builds one of me for another language.

**ANNA:** That is what I am. போலாமா?

[Pause: 1 sec]
