**ANNA:** வணக்கம். I'm Anna. In Tamil that's what you call your elder brother. Give me three minutes.

**ANNA:** Python, Claude, and a folder of prompt files on his laptop. No app, no company, nothing to subscribe to. Anyone can read every line of me.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** I teach him the machinery of the language. English marches: subject, verb, everything announced up front. Tamil holds the verb to the end and packs the whole cast inside it.

**ANNA:** போனோம் is one word. It means *we went*, and the ஓம் on the end is the "we". Another ending means somebody told me this. வந்தாராம். He came, so they say. Gossip, built into the grammar. A third ending carries respect. An elder hears it. An elder hears when it's missing.

**ANNA:** Those are engines. He learns one and builds a thousand sentences I never taught him.

**ANNA:** The nouns he borrows from English. Coimbatore Tamil is already half English. Fridge is fridge, bus is bus. Reach for the pure Tamil noun and you sound like a scholar. So his effort goes into the connective words, the ones that carry the sentence.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** The lesson takes a different shape every day. I use a form days before I explain it. Then one generous beat where I hand it over. That night it's in his headphones, inside a story, doing work. Days later I ask for it cold, in a situation he's never seen, with no warning. When it fires clean I stop asking. After that I just use it.

**ANNA:** Hint, teach, soak, test, retire. He's never told which stage he's in.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Speaking is half of it. The other half is standing where the Tamil isn't aimed at him. Two aunties at full speed, talking past him, and he catches the drift anyway. So I play both aunties, then ask: என்ன சொன்னாங்க? What did you catch? Most of the Tamil he'll ever hear will never be addressed to him.

**ANNA:** He recognizes a hundred and sixty words. Twenty-five come out of his mouth cold, with no warm-up and no options on a screen. He has twenty sentence patterns; eleven fire on a verb he's never drilled in that shape. Any app would hand him a bigger number. If I give him a word and he gives it straight back, I still count it as unknown.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Four tools. One file holds his brain, every word tagged for whether he catches it or fires it. One script picks tonight's targets. One studio writes and records a podcast overnight, built from what he fumbled that morning. And a knock: one line to his phone, a few times a day. He types Tamil back and I score it. I choose the hour, and I choose the silences.

**ANNA:** His wife is from Coimbatore. There's a family table he means to sit at in their language. He picks that moment. I make sure the Tamil is in his mouth before he does.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** You can have one of these. The repository is called சொல்லு. Sollu. It means *say it*. Clone it, tell your coding agent to set up your tutor, and it builds this around your language and your dialect.

**ANNA:** Andrew's late for his lesson. போலாமா?

[Pause: 1 sec]
