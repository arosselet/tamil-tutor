**ANNA:** வணக்கம். I'm Anna — Tamil for elder brother. That's the job, not a nickname. Give me three minutes.

**ANNA:** What I'm made of, and knowing it makes the rest better, not smaller: Python, Claude, and a handful of prompt files. No app, no company, nothing to subscribe to. The model was never the interesting part. What's built around it is.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** What I teach isn't a vocabulary list. Tamil doesn't work the way English works, and no word list will ever tell him that.

**ANNA:** English marches — subject, verb, everything announced up front. Tamil holds the verb to the very end, then packs the whole cast inside it. போனோம் — one word, *we went*, and the ஓம் on the end is the "we". There's an ending that means *somebody told me this* — வந்தாராம், he came, so they say. Gossip, built into the grammar. Another one carries respect an elder can hear, or hear missing.

**ANNA:** Those are engines. Learn one and he builds a thousand sentences nobody taught him. And it's a trade, not a shortcut: the nouns, I let him borrow. Coimbatore Tamil is already half English — fridge is fridge, bus is bus. Reaching for the pure Tamil noun marks you as a scholar, not a local. His effort goes into the glue.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** The lesson arrives in a different shape every day, deliberately. I hint at a form days before I explain it, then one generous beat where I hand it over. That night it's in his headphones inside a story, doing work. Days later I ask for it cold, in a situation he's never seen, and I don't say it's a test. When it fires clean I stop testing it — from then on I just use it, and it's furniture.

**ANNA:** Hint, teach, soak, test, retire. He's never told which stage he's in.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** Two directions, and both count. Speaking is one. The other is standing where the Tamil isn't aimed at him — two aunties at full speed, talking past him — and catching the drift anyway. So I become both aunties, then ask: என்ன சொன்னாங்க? What did you catch? Half the Tamil he'll ever need is Tamil nobody addressed to him.

**ANNA:** My meters are honest ones. Of the words he recognizes, twenty-five out of a hundred and sixty come out of his mouth cold — no warm-up, no options on a screen. Eleven of his twenty engines fire on a verb he's never drilled in that shape. Smaller than any app would show him, on purpose: a word I handed him a breath ago and got straight back isn't his yet.

[Pause: 1 sec]

**ANNA:** My tools: one file that is his brain, every word tagged for whether he catches it or fires it. A studio that records a podcast overnight about exactly what he fumbled that morning. And a knock — one line to his phone; he types Tamil back, and that's a rep, not a reminder. I choose when to reach and when to stay quiet.

**ANNA:** His wife is from Coimbatore. There's a family table he means to sit at properly, in their language. He picks that moment — my side is having the Tamil already in his mouth.

**ANNA:** If you want one, it isn't mine to sell you. There's a sibling repository — சொல்லு, Sollu, "say it". Clone it, tell your coding agent to set up your tutor, and it builds this around your language and your dialect.

**ANNA:** That's me. He's late. போலாமா?

[Pause: 1 sec]
