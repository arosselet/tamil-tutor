> **Karthi:** Priya came back just before the evening light went orange. The milk was in one hand, her bus bag in the other, and she had the look of somebody who had already had enough of the road.
> **Priya:** I got it. The shop had the last packet. The bus was so full I held this above my head the whole way.
> **Karthi:** You carried milk over your head on the bus?
> **Priya:** I carried it carefully. The man beside me carried a plant. The plant had more space than I did.

> **Karthi:** I laughed, but I had been waiting for her to come home. In the afternoon, she had asked if I could come along. An office call ran long. **poga mudiyala.** I couldn't go.
> **Priya:** You stayed home. The milk got here. Everything worked.
> **Karthi:** She said it lightly. I had already made a new plan in my head: tea at the little shop near the stop. She could tell me the rest of the bus story there.
> **Karthi:** **Bus-la polaam?** We can go by bus.
> **Priya:** The bus again?
> **Karthi:** There was a small pause. I could have said the tea was excellent. It is ordinary tea. I could have said the shop was quiet. It is never quiet. I wanted her to come with me, and I had made the plan sound like it was about tea.

> **Priya:** **Nee po. Naan inga irukken.**<br>You go. I'm staying here.
> **Karthi:** You go. I'm staying here. She said it kindly, like she was giving me the whole evening back. I picked up my keys. Then I saw her still standing by the gate, one sandal off, rubbing the mark the bus bag had left on her wrist.
> **Karthi:** You really want to stay?
> **Priya:** I really want to sit down. If you want tea, go. Don't wait for me.
> **Karthi:** That was fair. It was also not what I wanted. The milk was already home. There was no errand left to share, and no reason to turn tea into another bus ride.
> **Karthi:** **Sari.** I don't need the shop. Let's make tea here.
> **Priya:** You can make it. I carried the milk.
> **Karthi:** I put the milk on the counter. She sat down. The first cup was too strong, so she gave me hers and took mine.
> **Priya:** This one is better.
> **Karthi:** Of course. I made both cups.
> **Priya:** **Appadiyaa?** Then you can make tomorrow's too.
> **Karthi:** There was the little smile. She was tired, and she was still teasing me. I took the chair opposite her instead of the bus card from my pocket.
> **Karthi:** **Naanum inga irukken.** I'm here too.
> **Priya:** Good. The bus story is not finished.
> **Karthi:** It turned out the plant had a window seat. I had to hear that part twice.

> **Analyst Maya:** The tea shop was never really the plan, was it?
> **Analyst Raj:** No. When Karthi says **poga mudiyala**, he means he couldn't join her in the afternoon. It is the blocked plan that makes him reach for a second chance.
> **Analyst Maya:** Then comes **polaam** — “we can go.” He offers a bus ride, but the offer is really for company. The tea is the polite cover.
> **Analyst Raj:** She says **nee po. Naan inga irukken** — “you go; I'm here.” She is giving him the choice, and also telling him she is tired. He notices the mark from her bag and changes the plan.
> **Analyst Maya:** He answers **naanum inga irukken** — “I'm here too.” The errand is done; now what she wants is a chair and what he wants is her story. Tea at home gives them both.
> **Analyst Maya:** Which cup was actually better?
> **Analyst Raj:** The one she took. Obviously.
