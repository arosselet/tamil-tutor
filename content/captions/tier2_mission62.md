# Captions — Ep 62 · Idum & Paathukkaren: The Shrug and the Promise

> **Follow-along sheet** — the line as *sound* · what it means.
> Passes 1–2: listen with this open. Pass 3+: put it away — **blind is the win.**

*[SFX: Evening veranda — tea glasses clinking, a ceiling fan turning, distant street sound]*

> **Host B:** *Kalyanam kashtama irukku.*<br>The wedding is stressful.

> **Host A:** *Adhu sariyaayidum, maama.*<br>That'll sort itself out, uncle.

> **Host B:** *Enakku konjam bayama irukku.*<br>I'm a little scared.

> **Host A:** *Ellaam nalla aayidum.*<br>It'll all turn out fine.

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Host B:** *Indha saappaadu kaarama irukku. Enakku pidikkaadhu.*<br>This food is spicy. I don't like it.

> **Host A:** *Seekkiram pazhagiduveenga, maama.*<br>You'll get used to it quickly, uncle.

> **Host B:** *(tiredly) Naan tired-aa irukken. Aana nee eppavum ore maadhiri sollriyae — "aayidum, aayidum."*<br>I'm tired. But you always say the same thing — "it'll happen, it'll happen."

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Host A:** *(softly) Illa maama. Innum onnu irukku.*<br>No, uncle. There's one more thing.

> **Host A:** *Naan paathukkaren. Ellaathaiyum naan paathukkaren.*<br>I'll take care of it. I'll take care of everything.

*[Pause: 2 sec]*

*[SFX: Veranda sounds fade]*

> **Maya:** *Okay. That tea-table scene — every single worry, same response.*

> **Raj:** *Sariyaayidum. It'll sort itself out.*

> **Maya:** *The wedding's stressful — sariyaayidum. She's scared — aayidum. The food's too spicy — seekkiram pazhagidum.*

> **Raj:** *Not "I will fix it." Not even "someone will fix it." Just — it will happen. On its own.*

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya:** *Sari, let's take that apart. -idum. Where does that even come from?*

> **Raj:** *Idu. An old auxiliary verb — "to set down," "to finish off doing." Same family as vidu.*

> **Maya:** *Right — the completive. "I did the thing, and set it down. Done."*

> **Raj:** *Now bolt a future ending onto that completive. Mudinju plus idum. "It will have gotten itself finished."*

> **Maya:** *A done-deal future. Fated, almost.*

> **Raj:** *Aama. Book pannidum. Sollidum. Poidum. Any clean, single verb — it just slots on.*

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya:** *But it doesn't slot onto everything, does it.*

> **Raj:** *Illa. Only a clean single verb. "Kootittu varo" — a compound, two verbs stuck together — you can't staple -idum onto that.*

> **Maya:** *So what happens to the messy ones?*

> **Raj:** *That's exactly where paathukkaren steps in.*

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya:** *Which is the opposite move, actually. Idum removes the person from the sentence. Paathukkaren puts a person right back in the middle of it.*

> **Raj:** *Naan paathukkaren. Not "it'll happen." "I will personally see to it."*<br>I'll take care of it.

> **Maya:** *Paar — to look, to see. Plus thukka — doing it for yourself, on yourself.*

> **Raj:** *"I will look after — for myself — to it." That's the literal shape.*

> **Maya:** *Azhagaana vaarthai, that. The whole ethic of taking responsibility, sitting right inside the grammar.*<br>A beautiful word, that.

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj:** *(quieter) Can I tell you something, Maya?*

> **Maya:** *Enna aachu?*<br>What happened?

> **Raj:** *That vignette — that's exactly how my grandmother used to sound. En paatti.*<br>My grandmother.

> **Maya:** *Sariyaayidum?*<br>"It'll sort itself out"?

> **Raj:** *Aama. Every worry, every single evening — she'd wave it off the same way. Sariyaayidum.*<br>Yes. ...It'll sort itself out.

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya:** *She said that to you.*

> **Raj:** *Ella naalum sonnaanga. Every single day of my childhood.*<br>She said it every day.

*[Pause: 2 sec]*

> **Maya:** *(gently) She's not around anymore, is she.*

> **Raj:** *Illa. Four years now.*<br>No.

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj:** *Aana — in the last year, something changed. She couldn't say "sariyaayidum" anymore and mean it.*

> **Maya:** *So what did she say instead?*

> **Raj:** *She stopped shrugging. En amma kitta oru naal sonnaanga — "Naan paathukkaren."*<br>She said to my mother, one day — "I'll take care of it."

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya:** *She moved from letting go — to holding on.*

> **Raj:** *Aama. From "it'll fix itself" to "I will fix it." Right at the end.*

*[Pause: 2 sec]*

> **Maya:** *And now?*

> **Raj:** *(small laugh) Ippo naan thaan adhu solra aalu. Last month, my mother's knee was hurting, worrying about a doctor's bill —*<br>Now I'm the one who says it.

> **Raj:** *and it just came out of me. "Amma, naan paathukkaren."*<br>"Mom, I'll take care of it."

> **Maya:** *The word found you.*

> **Raj:** *It waited for me, actually.*

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya:** *So somewhere in that tea-table scene we heard — someone grows up to say the same word, one day.*

> **Raj:** *Eppavum appaditaan nadakkum. The shrug runs out. Someone always has to actually pick it up.*<br>It always happens that way.

> **Maya:** *Idum lets you rest. Paathukkaren is what you say when you're done resting.*

> **Raj:** *(quiet, warm) Azhagaana, illaiyaa?*<br>Beautiful, isn't it?

> **Maya:** *Aama. A beautiful shift.*<br>Yes.

*[Pause: 1 sec]*

*[SFX: Evening veranda ambiance briefly returns — a glass set down gently — then fades]*
