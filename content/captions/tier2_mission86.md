# Captions — Ep 86 · The Family That Never Decides

> **Follow-along sheet** — the line as *sound* · what it means.
> Passes 1–2: listen with this open. Pass 3+: put it away — **blind is the win.**

*[SFX: A ceiling fan turning slowly. A cricket commentary murmuring from a TV in the next room. Somewhere behind it, a suitcase zip.]*

> **Host B (M):** *Neram aachu. **Kilambalaamaa?***<br>It's time. Shall we leave?

> **Host A (F):** *Aama aama. Kilambalaam.*<br>Yeah, yeah. We can leave.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Host A (F):** *Aana... ippo traffic romba jaasti.*<br>But... right now the traffic is way too much.

> **Host B (M):** *Jaastiyaa?*<br>Too much?

> **Host A (F):** ***Mudinjaa** innum **pathu** nimisham. Appuram kilambalaam.*<br>If possible, ten more minutes. Then we can leave.

> **Host B (M):** *Sari. **Utkaaralaamaa?***<br>Okay. Shall we sit?

> **Host A (F):** *Maama enna solraanga theriyumaa?*<br>Do you know what Uncle is saying?

> **Host B (M):** *Enna?*<br>What?

> **Host A (F):** *Mudhalla saapidalaam, **appuram paakkalaam**-nu.*<br>First we can eat — we'll see later, he says.

> **Host B (M):** *Enakku **vayiru full**-aa irukku. **Neraa** kilambalaam.*<br>My stomach is full. Let's leave straight away.

> **Host A (F):** *Konjam **pesalaamaa**? Bags door **pakkam** irukku. Avasaram illa.*<br>Shall we talk a bit? The bags are by the door. There's no hurry.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Host B (M):** *Atthai enna sonnaanga?*<br>What did Aunty say?

> **Host A (F):** ***Kadaisila paakkalaam**-nu sonnaanga.*<br>She said we'll see at the end.

> **Host B (M):** *...Kadaisila.*<br>...At the end.

*[SFX: A suitcase lifted off the floor. Two footsteps. A door. Out on the road, one auto horn.]*

> **Host A (F):** *Atthai **petti** eduthuttaanga.*<br>Aunty has picked up the suitcase.

> **Host B (M):** *Avanga veliya poraanga!*<br>She's going outside!

> **Host A (F):** *Ayyo. **Seekkiram kilambunga**!*<br>Oh no. Leave, quickly!

> *[Pause: 2 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** Eight suggestions. Zero decisions.

> **Raj (M):** And one aunt with a suitcase. That is a Coimbatore departure, start to finish.

> **Maya (F):** Nobody in that hall said no, Raj. Did you notice that?

> **Raj (M):** Nobody said yes either. That's the whole trick.

> **Maya (F):** And the entire thing ran on one tail. One. ***laam.***

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj (M):** Say it on its own, Maya. ***laam.***

> **Maya (F):** ***Laam.***

> **Raj (M):** Now watch what it eats.

> **Maya (F):** Take *kilambu.* To leave, to set off.

> **Raj (M):** ***Kilamba-laam.*** We can leave.

> **Maya (F):** Take *saapidu.* To eat.

> **Raj (M):** ***Saapida-laam.*** We can eat.

> **Maya (F):** Take *po.* To go. ***Po-laam.***

> **Raj (M):** *Utkaaru*, to sit. ***Utkaaralaam.***

> **Maya (F):** *Pesu*, to talk. ***Pesalaam.***

> **Raj (M):** The verb keeps changing. ***Laam*** never changes.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** And notice what it refuses to do. It never orders anybody.

> **Raj (M):** It only says: this is possible.

> **Maya (F):** Somebody else can decide. Not me. I only mentioned it.

> **Raj (M):** Then there's one more sound, and it changes the job completely.

> **Maya (F):** ***Aa.*** Right at the end.

> **Raj (M):** *Kilambalaam* becomes ***Kilambalaamaa?*** Shall we leave?

> **Maya (F):** *Utkaaralaam* becomes ***Utkaaralaamaa?*** Shall we sit?

> **Raj (M):** *Pesalaam* becomes ***Pesalaamaa?***

> **Maya (F):** Verb plus ***laam*** is a suggestion. Plus ***aa***, and it's a question.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj (M):** My brother's boy found a third job for it. He came to the door yesterday and asked me, *"Naan veliya **polaamaa?**"*<br>"May I go outside?"

> **Maya (F):** May I go outside. Same tail — now it's permission.

> **Raj (M):** Suggestion, question, permission. One machine, three jobs.

> **Maya (F):** And in that hall this morning it had a fourth one. This is my favourite.

> **Raj (M):** The wall.

> **Maya (F):** ***Appuram paakkalaam.*** We'll see later.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj (M):** It sounds like an open door. It's a closed one.

> **Maya (F):** She wanted to stay home. She never once said she wanted to stay home.

> **Raj (M):** She said traffic. She said food. She said the bags are near the door.

> **Maya (F):** Every reason was about somebody else's comfort. Flawless.

> **Raj (M):** And he wanted to be gone twenty minutes earlier. He never said that either.

> **Maya (F):** He said ***neraa kilambalaam.*** Straight out, let's go. Polite as anything.

> **Raj (M):** Two people, opposite plans, and not one rude word between them.

> **Maya (F):** ***Laam*** did all the fighting for both of them.

> **Raj (M):** And nobody moved for forty minutes. That's the ***alaichal***, Maya. Not the traffic. The hall.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** So how does a family like that ever get out of the house?

> **Raj (M):** Somebody picks up a bag. That's it. That's the decision.

> **Maya (F):** The aunt. Who proposed nothing all morning.

> **Raj (M):** In a Coimbatore house the decision is never announced. It's carried out the door.

> *[Pause: 2 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** Now. The word I actually want to open up. ***Paarkkalaam.***

> **Raj (M):** The polite escape hatch of the entire language.

> **Maya (F):** Its root is ***paar.*** To look. To see.

> **Raj (M):** ***Paarthen.*** I saw. The same ***paar***, sitting right at the front.

> **Maya (F):** So ***paarkkalaam*** is, literally, we may see. Nothing is decided. Only the looking is promised.

> **Raj (M):** English does exactly the same thing. "We'll see."

> **Maya (F):** Has "we'll see" ever once meant yes? In the history of families?

> **Raj (M):** Never. Not one time.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** And the one she sent out last — ***kadaisila paakkalaam.***

> **Raj (M):** We'll see at the end. The verdict that decides nothing.

> **Maya (F):** Hear what the mouth does to the *r* there. Careful and slow, it's ***paarkkalaam.***

> **Raj (M):** Fast, in a doorway, with a bag in one hand — ***paakkalaam.***

> **Maya (F):** ***Appuram paakkalaam. Kadaisila paakkalaam.***

> **Raj (M):** Nobody has time for that *r* when the auto is waiting.

> **Maya (F):** But the long *aa* stays. It always stays. And that *aa* is about to earn its keep.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj (M):** Because there's a word that lives next door to it and means something completely different.

> **Maya (F):** ***Pakkam.*** Side. Direction.

> **Raj (M):** Short. Flat. ***Pakkam.***

> **Maya (F):** *Onnu*, ***rendu.*** Two beats and it's finished.

> **Raj (M):** No long *aa.* And no ***paar*** anywhere inside it.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** ***Pakkam.***

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj (M):** ***Paakkalaam.***

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** ***Pakkam.***

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Raj (M):** ***Paakkalaam.***

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** One of them is a place. The other one is a plan.

> **Raj (M):** Put both of them in an auto and the difference gets very loud.

> **Maya (F):** Go on.

> **Raj (M):** Somebody gets in and says, *"temple **pakkathula** niruthunga."*<br>"Stop beside the temple."

> **Maya (F):** Stop beside the temple. That's a place. The driver stops. Everybody is happy.

> **Raj (M):** Now the same person says, *"temple **paakkalaam**."*<br>"Let's go and see a temple."

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** Ah. Let's go and look at a temple.

> **Raj (M):** That's not a stop. That's an outing.

> **Maya (F):** He smiles, he takes the long road, and thirty minutes later somebody is standing in front of a temple wondering how they got there.

> **Raj (M):** And the meter is still running.

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** So — a place is ***pakkam.*** Short, flat, no *aa.*

> **Raj (M):** A plan is ***paakkalaam.*** Long, with ***paar*** hiding at the front of it.

> **Maya (F):** And when the word wanted is "beside", the useful one is ***pakkathula.***

> **Raj (M):** Bus stop ***pakkathula.*** Hotel ***pakkathula.***

> **Maya (F):** Same ***pakkam***, with the *-la* tail on it. In, at, beside.

> **Raj (M):** One tail turns a place into a location. Another tail turns a verb into a suggestion.

> **Maya (F):** Tamil is mostly tails, Raj. The words just come along for the ride.

> *[Pause: 2 sec]*

> **Raj (M):** Right. It's nearly four o'clock. Coffee ***kudikkalaamaa?***<br>Shall we drink a coffee?

> *[Pause: 1 sec]*

> **Maya (F):** *...**Kadaisila paakkalaam.***<br>...We'll see at the end.

> **Raj (M):** Ayyo. And that is exactly how it starts.

*[SFX: The ceiling fan again, the cricket commentary swelling for a moment, then a door closing.]*
