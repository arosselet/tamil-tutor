The Tails — who did it · the last syllable IS the pronoun

●-én = I saaptén I ate · varén I'm coming · sonnén I said
●-aanga = they / one elder vandhaanga they came · sonnaanga they said
●-chu = it aachu it happened · mudinjuchu it wrapped up
◐-om = we ponom we went · saaptom we ate — the trip is a “we” trip
○-nga = polite you sollunga please tell · ukkarunga please sit
The law: a -ttu / -i word (saapittu, poyittu) is a comma — “having done that…”. The tail is the period. Land every sentence on a tail: the wind-up needs the throw.

Must & Can Machines

◐verb + num must ___ poganum must go · pannanum must do · sollanum must say
○place-ku poganum need to go to ___ Coimbatore-ku poganum
◐verb + mudiyala can't ___ pooga mudiyala can't go · mudiyaadhu — no chance, ever
◐verb + idum it'll sort itself sariyaayidum it'll be fine · hands-off future
Root lore — mudi = finish. mudinjuchu “it finished” · mudichittén “I finished it” · mudiyum “can” (it'll come to completion) · mudiyaadhu “can't” (it never will). Tamil doesn't say you lack ability — it says the thing won't finish itself.

The Register Rule · one rule covers the whole family

When in doubt: -nga. Everyone gets neenga / -nga forms — elders, strangers, even younger relatives. nee is only for genuinely close cousins and siblings. The safe register is also the correct one in this family. Bonus: vandhaanga might be a crowd, or just one respected elder — deference is baked into the grammar.
