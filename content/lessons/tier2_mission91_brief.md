# Tier 2, Mission 91 — Ten Minutes Before the Car

## Core Targets
- **Linguistic Pattern:** The Verb Under Load — same root fired twice, once as the thing (noun bait) and once as the doing (verb, the only correct answer); one axis only, no person-stacking, no -nga added to the contrast.
- **Register:** grief/nostalgia
- **Dramatic Ingredient:** stakes
- **Scenario Shape:** debrief
- **Location class:** kitchen
- **Energy:** loud
- **Episode Form:** phone_call

## Scenario Context
The car to the airport is coming in ten minutes and athai is still at the stove. Her phone is wedged against her shoulder — her sister back in the ooru, checking in before the flight — while four people orbit the kitchen at once, each one naming a dish and getting an answer about what her hands are doing to it instead. This exact scramble happens every single visit, the last ten minutes before every goodbye, and it never gets easier; she says so once into the phone, briefly, between fielding the room. The stakes are plain: if the rice isn't moving and the bags aren't zipped, somebody misses the flight.

## Word Payload

**NEW (4 items):**
- **சமைக்கிற** (*samaikkira*) — cooking / is cooking — THE PAYLOAD. Never let the noun answer for it.
- **என்ன சமைக்கிற?** (*enna samaikkira*) — What are you cooking? (informal) — the direct carrier; he's slipped to the noun on this exact chunk three times (nga-address log, 09-11). The line only resolves when the reply is the verb.
- **குடுக்கிற** (*kudukkira*) — giving / is giving — a second engine under the same axis, so the pattern reads as a machine, not a vocabulary item.
- **பாக்கிற** (*paakkira*) — looking / checking — a third engine; he already owns பார்த்தேன், so the root is familiar even though this tense isn't.

**Noun traps (bait only — never the answer, never glossed as a "lesson"):**
- சமையல் (*samayal*) — the cooking / the dish
- காப்பி (*kaapi*) — coffee
- வேலை (*velai*) — work / the task

Build every payload beat so one of these three is the lazy, obvious thing to say — and the line only works when a person answers with what they're *doing* to it instead.

**CALLBACKS (4 items):**
- **வந்தேன்** — I came [struggled] — the sister/relative arriving, or the car arriving
- **கன்ஃபர்ம்** — Confirm [struggled] — natural on a phone call about a flight ("time confirm பண்ணீங்களா?")
- **கிடைச்சுது** — Received / Got it [struggled] — did the sweets/packet make it into the bag
- **சின்ன** — Small / Little [struggled] — portion size, "a small tumbler," packed-bag size

Soft target 2–3 landed naturally; don't bend the scene to force all four.

**Ear catches (weave in — recognition only, never cold-quizzed):**
- frame:cant-முடியல் (தூக்க முடியல், போக முடியல்) — fits the packing/departure pressure directly
- frame:myself-thukka (பாத்துக்கறேன்) — athai reassuring the room/the phone that she's got it
- இன்னும் கொஞ்சம் சாப்பிடுங்க + the refusal pair (வேண்டாம்மா, வயிறு நிறைஞ்சிடுச்சு) — someone being pressed to eat before they go, someone refusing because there's no time
- சும்மா சொல்றாங்க — if the phone call carries any gossip aside

## Notes

This episode is a repair commissioned off a live slip pattern, not a fresh topic — treat the surface story (the goodbye scramble) as the whole content; nothing in the audio may name a rule, say "noun" or "verb," or acknowledge that a pattern is being corrected. No counts, no tally, no cue in the intro/outro about what he's gotten wrong. He is doing reps inside a story, not reading a report.

Keep the contrast to one axis: root shown twice (thing vs. doing), always in first- or informal-second-person present tense, never permuted across persons and never with -nga stacked onto the payload verbs themselves (-nga can live elsewhere in the scene — e.g. on callback material — just not fused onto சமைக்கிற/குடுக்கிற/பாக்கிற in the contrast beats).

The fourth wall stays up throughout — hosts don't know there's a learner; the "he keeps missing this" framing is for the Architect's build, never for the room.
