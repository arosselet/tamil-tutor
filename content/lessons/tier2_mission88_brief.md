# Tier 2, Mission 88 — The Disaster Errand

## Core Targets
- **Linguistic Pattern:** The `-ல` Negative Machine (Verbal Negation)
- **Register:** tenderness
- **Dramatic Ingredient:** genre (a gentle "comedy of errors" / farcical disaster)
- **Scenario Shape:** debrief
- **Location class:** home_social
- **Energy:** medium
- **Episode Form:** classic

## Scenario Context
An errand into town has gone spectacularly wrong. The shop was shut, the phone wouldn't connect, and the contact person's name was forgotten. One person is recounting the chaos to a loved one. Despite the "hassle" (alaichal), there is no frustration—only the tender, laughing realization that nothing went to plan today. Every beat of the story is punctuated by something that "didn't happen" or "couldn't work."

## Word Payload

**NEW (3 words):**
- **புரியல** (*puriyala*) — I don't understand (The "keep-going" vs "giving-up" marker)
- **முடியல** (*mudiyala*) — I can't / It's not possible (Physical or situational inability)
- **தெரியல** (*theriyala*) — I don't know (Situational ignorance/uncertainty)

**CALLBACKS (5 words):**
- **அலைச்சல்** (*alaichal*) — The hassle / the running-around [recently-mastered]
- **என்ன சொன்னீங்க?** (*enna sonneenga?*) — What did you say? [floor-gap]
- **சும்மா சொல்றாங்க** (*summa solraanga*) — They're just talking / making it up [due]
- **அப்படியா?!** (*appadiya*) — Oh really?! [comfortable]
- **ஃப்ரீயா விடுங்க** (*freeya vidunga*) — Don't worry / let it go [struggled]

## Notes
The focus is the `-ல` suffix as a "machine" that negates the verb. The learner knows *puriyala* and *mudiyala* as frozen chunks; this mission must reveal they are built from the same engine. Contrast *puriyala* (closing the attempt) with *enna sonneenga?* (requesting a retry) to show the conversational utility. The "tenderness" register ensures the "disaster" feels like a shared joke rather than a complaint.
