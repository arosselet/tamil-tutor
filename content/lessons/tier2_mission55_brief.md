## Scenario Context

Maya and Raj open with a brief (~30-second) specimen vignette from a Coimbatore tea stall: one host orders with வேணும், the other refuses with வேண்டாம் and counter-requests. The two forms of the root land back-to-back in 10 seconds. Maya and Raj then dissect the specimen end-to-end as the lore hosts they are.

The mischief is in the reveal: Tamil has one word (வேணும்) where English insists on two (want / need) — and the language never bothered making that moral distinction. The turn arrives mid-discussion when Raj introduces the grandmother's dinner-table command ("நீ சாப்பிட வேணும்!"), which uses the exact same syllables as the tea order but carries categorical moral weight. After the turn, every prior mention of வேணும் lands differently. The episode closes on the practical power of that clean root at any stall or table.

## Word Payload

**NEW (1 word told deep — the protagonist):**
- **frame:want-noun** — {noun} வேணும் → I want {noun} / {noun} is needed. Literary form: வேண்டும். Spoken Coimbatore: வேணும் (ண்ட cluster collapses). Negation mirror: வேண்டாம் (I don't want / not needed). Root வேண்டு in Classical Tamil / Sangam poetry. The defining lore: Tamil refuses the want/need moral hierarchy English built — one word covers the full spectrum from casual desire to moral necessity; context carries the weight.
