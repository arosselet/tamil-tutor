# Tier 2, Mission 92 — The Cousin on the Line

## Core Targets
- **Linguistic Pattern:** The Verb-Carried Answer (the noun is the easy out)
- **Register:** delight
- **Dramatic Ingredient:** subtext — two people want opposite things under polite words
- **Scenario Shape:** gossip
- **Location class:** home_social
- **Energy:** low
- **Episode Form:** story

## Scenario Context

Evening, home phone rings. A cousin from three streets over — nosy, affectionate, angling for a dinner invitation she'll never ask for outright — wants to know what's cooking before she decides whether to "just happen to drop by." The whole call is velvet-glove interrogation: she circles the same question from every direction, and the easy move each time is to name the dish instead of naming what's actually being done to it. Let that pattern repeat at least three times across the call — asked, answered, needled back when the answer slides toward a dish name instead of the doing-word — so by the last pass it comes out clean and complete on its own.

Tell this as a story, not a live call: one host recounting the exchange afterward, replaying the cousin's lines and his own with visible amusement at how transparent she was and how long he pretended not to notice. The comedy is in the mismatch — she wants an invitation, he wants her off the phone before the food's actually ready — both sides staying perfectly pleasant the entire time.

**Hard constraints from the commission — binding on the script itself:** carry the full question verbatim (không paraphrased) at least three times, asked and answered. Never name a rule, never say the words "noun" or "verb" anywhere in the episode. No meta-commentary in intro, outro, or anywhere in-scene about mistakes or correction. No counts spoken aloud. One grammatical axis only — do not stack a polite-register (-nga) drill or a person-toggle drill on top of this; the verb-carries-the-question pattern stands alone.

## Word Payload

**NEW (4 items):**
- **கை ருசி** (*kai ruchi*) — a cook's signature touch, lit. "hand's taste" (chunk — covers the body cluster's கை and the home_kitchen cluster's ருசி in one culturally-loaded unit; the cousin's compliment/bait line)
- **இது என்ன?** (*idhu enna?*) — What is this? (questions cluster; a natural contrast beat against the payload question — she points at something else on the side and asks this instead, without the scene ever naming why the two questions behave differently)
- **எதுக்கு?** (*edhukku?*) — For what? / Why? (questions cluster; the prying follow-up)
- **வாய்** (*vaai*) — mouth (body cluster; lands naturally in food-talk — "வாய்க்கு ருசியா இருக்கும்" register)

**CALLBACKS (5 items):**
- **என்ன சமைக்கிற?** (*enna samaikkira?*) — What are you cooking? (informal) — **[COMMISSIONED, lead payload]** slipped 3× (noun-for-verb); hinted→cold, 5 reps. Must fire verbatim ≥3× across the call, each time answered so the doing-word carries the line.
- **இருக்கார்ல** (*irukkaarla*) — you know (he) is there, right… — the gossip opener; slipped once (honorific-verb); hinted→cold. A natural fit for the cousin building up to her real question ("நம்ம மாமா இருக்கார்ல…").
- **வந்தேன்** (*vandhen*) — I came — struggled, due callback. Fits as his timing excuse ("இப்போ தான் வந்தேன்").
- **frame:cant-முடியல்** — {verb}+முடியல் → can't {verb} (e.g. பேச முடியல், இப்போ சொல்ல முடியல்) — struggled, due callback/machine. Usable as a deflection elsewhere in the call, kept clear of the three payload reps.
- **சின்ன** (*chinna*) — small/little — struggled, due callback; soft target only, let it in if it fits a line naturally (e.g. downplaying how much cooking is really happening).

## Notes

This is a re-cut of the stalled M91 order: the athai-kitchen frame didn't carry the payload cleanly, so the whole chunk moves to a lighter, funnier venue (a cousin's phone call) where the noun-trap has room to repeat three clean times without feeling drilled. Keep the meal-serving ops (வேண்டாம்/நீங்க சாப்பிட்டீங்களா) out of this scene entirely — they're retired for now, aimed at a table he isn't sitting at; this is a phone call, not a meal. Do not let வா appear in any spoken production line — it stays held out unannounced for a future ambush. Register is delight and the ingredient is subtext, so let the pleasant surface do the comedic work; nobody in the scene should ever sound like they're teaching or correcting anyone.
