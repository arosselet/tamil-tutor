The sounds this sheet tests (read once)
ழ zh: the curled-back hover
Curl your tongue tip far back, past the ridge behind your teeth, toward the roof of your mouth, and let it hover without touching. Voiced and continuous, like a deep American r. Wrong: an English l, with the tip flat on the ridge.
ர · ற r: one light tap, either letter
Your wife's ruling (07-31): Coimbatore speakers mostly make no difference between ர and ற (or ல and ள). Both are one light tap of the tongue tip. Wrong, two ways: an American r (tongue bunched, touching nothing), or a hard trilled ற, which is the over-articulation that made the TTS sound uncanny to her.
ண · ட N, T: retroflex (tongue curled back)
Curl the tip back and touch the roof behind the ridge. Doubled (ண்ண, ட்ட) means hold the contact for a beat. Wrong: an English n or t, tip on the ridge with no curl.
த th: dental
Tip pressed on the back of your top teeth, soft, no puff of air. It is NOT the English th in "thin" or "the" (the romanized "th" invites exactly that), and NOT an English t.
Long vowels: aa, ee, oo, oh
Hold them about twice as long as feels natural; aa as in "father". A short a is like the u in "cup". Length changes words: padam (film) / paadam (lesson), kaal (leg) / kal (stone).
Doubled consonants
ll, kk, tt, pp: stay on the sound a beat, or stop briefly before releasing.
Book Tamil and unfused words
The written forms (pogiren, varugiradhu) and dictionary-word-by-word delivery (enna. aachu.) are what your nephew called book Tamil. Spoken Coimbatore contracts and fuses: poren, ennaachu.
